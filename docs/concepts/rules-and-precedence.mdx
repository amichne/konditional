---
title: "Rules and precedence"
---

A rule pairs a value with at most one condition. A rule with no condition, declared with `always` or `enable`, applies to every context.

## Specificity

Specificity is the number of distinct comparisons a context must satisfy.

- A fact comparison scores one.
- `allOf` scores the number of distinct comparisons among its terms. A comparison that appears twice counts once.
- `anyOf` takes the highest score among its terms.
- `not` scores the same as its operand.
- A label from `named` and the nesting of one `allOf` inside another do not change the score.

`allOf(a, b)`, `allOf(a, allOf(a, b))`, and `named("policy", allOf(b, a, a))` all score two.
You can extract a condition into a helper or reuse it in two policies without changing which rule wins.
A rule with no condition scores zero.

The context determines whether the expression matches. Its score depends only on the expression.

## Selection

The engine considers rules in descending order of specificity. Rules with equal scores keep their declaration order.
For each match, the engine checks rollout eligibility. A rule that misses its rollout does not prevent a later rule from matching.

The first eligible match supplies the value. If no rule qualifies, evaluation returns the feature default. Use [`explain`](/reference/evaluation-diagnostics) to see the decision for a specific context.
